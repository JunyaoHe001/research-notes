## Abstract

This thesis examines how social media reconfigures power in collaborative planning. Case studies from Beijing and Guangzhou show that digital platforms can expand participation, resource mobilisation, and public contestation while preserving existing asymmetries and creating new forms of platform-mediated and institutional control. The thesis connects collaborative planning theory, network power, and computational methods to develop a relational framework for measuring how power is formed, circulated, and translated into planning influence.
