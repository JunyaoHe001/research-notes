# Digital Nomad Mobility

This project studies digital-nomad mobility as an emerging form of digitally enabled urban mobility. It asks how a global city network is formed through individual movement, which cities occupy central or intermediary positions, and whether the resulting inequalities differ from established urban and migration hierarchies.

## Candidate mechanisms

- Geographic arbitrage between income and living costs
- Remote-work infrastructure and internet quality
- Visa and regulatory accessibility
- Urban amenities, climate, and environmental conditions
- Existing tourism, migration, and knowledge networks
- Agglomeration and cumulative network effects

## Analytical direction

The first stage reconstructs temporal city-to-city mobility networks and measures their hierarchy, concentration, communities, and evolution. The second stage tests proposed drivers through causal or hypothesis-based models. A final stage examines nonlinearities and heterogeneous effects using interpretable machine learning.
