# Hybrid Work and Job–Home Networks

This research examines whether different intensities of hybrid work generate distinct job–home network structures. The focus is on how residential origins and employment destinations become connected across space, and how these relations vary in reach, diversity, hierarchy, concentration, and inequality.

## Main questions

1. How does the national job–home network vary with hybrid-work intensity?
2. How is hybrid-work intensity associated with partner coverage, partner diversity, and mean external distance?
3. How do these associations differ by settlement context and existing network hierarchy?

## Analytical direction

The project combines multilayer network analysis with district-level panel modelling. Residential and employment roles are analysed separately to identify whether spatial flexibility expands labour-market reach symmetrically or produces different effects at the two ends of the job–home relation.
