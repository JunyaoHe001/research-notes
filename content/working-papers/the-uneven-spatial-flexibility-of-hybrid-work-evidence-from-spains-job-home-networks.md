title: "The Uneven Spatial Flexibility of Hybrid Work: Evidence from Spain’s
  Job–Home Networks"
authors:
  - He, J
  - Yang, C.
year: 2026
publication-type: Working paper
status: Submitted
research-line: Hybrid work, job–home networks, and regional restructuring
description: Job-home linkages connect residential locations to employment
  centers and constitute a relational structure within urban systems. Hybrid
  work reduces how often these linkages require travel to a fixed workplace, but
  it remains unclear whether varying degrees of flexibility produce distinct
  spatial networks. Using district-level mobility data for Spain from 2022 to
  2024, this article develops a gradient network approach that compares national
  structures, traces district-level relational change, and examines
  heterogeneity by settlement urbanity and established network position. The
  results show that flexibility differentiates job-home networks rather than
  simply reducing conventional commuting. Toward the flexible end of the
  gradient, networks become broader and less concentrated, with the strongest
  geographic extension appearing among the most flexible connections. Within
  districts, hybrid intensity is associated most strongly with a more balanced
  distribution of partners, and the associations are consistently larger for
  employment catchments than for residential employment fields. These changes
  remain spatially uneven. Urban districts experience stronger diversification,
  whereas districts with weaker established positions expand more rapidly but
  remain below established centers. Hybrid work thus broadens the geography of
  labor-market matching while preserving the urban hierarchy through which
  employment and network advantages are organized. The study contributes a
  relational framework for understanding hybrid work as a reorganization of
  urban systems shaped simultaneously by network expansion, directional
  asymmetry, and persistent spatial inequality.
